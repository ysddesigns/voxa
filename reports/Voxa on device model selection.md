# Build Voxa on sherpa-onnx, then measure

Voxa's best-fit foundation is **one native runtime, sherpa-onnx (Apache-2.0), carrying small int8 models on every tier**. That means a streaming Zipformer transducer (about 42–70 MB) for "Listen", pyannote-3.0 plus TitaNet-small diarization (about 0.11 RTF on one desktop thread) for speaker labels, and a Kitten or licence-clean Piper voice (about 25–80 MB) for "Speak for me". Around that core sit platform OCR (bundled ML Kit Latin on Android, Apple Vision on iOS), TextRank-style extractive summaries on every phone, and a 0.8–2B GGUF LLM through llama.rn for Smart summaries on 4 GB+ phones. The risks are less about model choice than about three facts the notes make plain. First, **sherpa-onnx's prebuilt mobile libraries statically link GPL-3.0 espeak-ng**, whichever voice you load. Second, **the best Nigeria-matched data (AfriSpeech-200, NaijaVoices) and the only ready Yoruba/Hausa/Igbo phone models (Meta MMS) are non-commercial**. Third, **no source measured any of these models on a 2 GB Android 8 phone**: every Lite-tier speed figure is extrapolated from a Galaxy S10, an emulator, an M3 or a desktop CPU. Accuracy is the other hard truth. Off-the-shelf ASR scores 26–33% WER on Nigerian English, against about 2% on LibriSpeech, so accent fine-tuning is product work, not polish. Nigeria is about 87% Android, dominated by Transsion's entry phones, and data costs ₦500–800 per GB. So Voxa should bundle only Lite models, offer bigger ones as Wi-Fi downloads, and treat a four-to-six-week device benchmark on real itel, Tecno and Redmi handsets as the gate before locking any model.

## Streaming Zipformer anchors "Listen"; Nigerian accents cost 13–20x in WER

The Lite-tier ASR choice is the sherpa-onnx streaming Zipformer transducer. The 20M English model is **about 42 MB int8 with RTF 0.038 on one desktop thread**. The 2023-06-26 model's encoder is 68 MB int8 (RTF 0.062), and the GigaSpeech-trained 2023-06-21 model's encoder is 179 MB int8 ([sherpa-onnx Zipformer models](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/pretrained_models/online-transducer/zipformer-transducer-models.html)). These are true streaming models with built-in endpointing, so partial text appears as people speak. Low-end Cortex-A53/A55 cores are perhaps 5–10x slower than the desktop reference, which puts a plausible phone RTF at 0.2–0.6. That is an estimate, not a measurement, and it is the single most important number to verify.

Two 2026 releases raise the ceiling for better phones. **Moonshine v2 Streaming** (Tiny 34M, Small 123M, Medium 245M) gets 7.84% average Open ASR WER for Small, with 148 ms response latency on an Apple M3, against 1,940 ms for Whisper Small ([Moonshine v2 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12241)). A React Native binding, `@siteed/moonshine.rn`, exists but was not evaluated ([jsDelivr](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@siteed/moonshine.rn@0.4.0/README.md)), and the v2 licence text is unconfirmed. NeMo's 114M cache-aware FastConformer streaming model reaches 5.7 WER on LibriSpeech test-other at 480 ms look-ahead ([NVIDIA](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/stt_en_fastconformer_hybrid_large_streaming_multi)). At the top end, **Nemotron Speech Streaming 0.6B** scores 7.07% average WER at 0.56 s chunks, has built-in punctuation and casing, and is licensed for commercial use under the NVIDIA Open Model License ([NVIDIA](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-speech-streaming-en-0.6b)). Microsoft's April 2026 study calls its int4 build (0.67 GB, 8.20% WER) the strongest real-time English streaming candidate for constrained hardware, but it measured only a 32-core server ([arXiv 2604.14493](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14493v2)). That size makes it a Pro-only, Wi-Fi-only download.

Whisper is the wrong live engine for Android. It only pseudo-streams through VAD slices, and its fixed 30 s window wastes compute on short slices. whisper.rn is mature (MIT, CoreML, Parakeet support). But its RealtimeTranscriber keeps every slice result in memory by default, so a 60-minute meeting needs explicit `maxResultsInMemory` caps ([whisper.rn](https://github.com/mybigday/whisper.rn)). Its right role is an optional post-meeting re-pass on Pro devices. Parakeet TDT 0.6B is accurate in batch mode, but chunked streaming lifts its average WER from 6.32% to 12.83% ([arXiv 2604.14493](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14493v2)). OS engines cannot be primary either. Android's on-device recognizer needs API 31+ and is officially "not intended to be used for continuous recognition" ([Android](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/speech/SpeechRecognizer)). Apple's iOS 26 SpeechTranscriber adds no app size and handles long-form audio, but it reports unavailable on some iPhone 11 Pro units ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/806765)). It is a good free bonus on newer iPhones and nothing more.

Accent is the larger problem than engine choice. In AfriSpeech-MultiBench, **Whisper-large-v3 scores 2.01% WER on LibriSpeech but 26.53% on Nigerian speech, and Parakeet-0.6B goes from 1.69% to 32.60%**. Names and numbers push most models above 40%, and several models hallucinate badly on silence ([arXiv 2511.14255](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.14255v1)). Nobody has published Nigerian-English WER for Zipformer, Moonshine or Whisper tiny/base. Fine-tuning works, though. Whisper-small on AfriSpeech-200 fell from 49.15% to 22.94% WER ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Professor/whisper-small-afrispeech)), and Intron's proprietary Sahara V2 reaches 12.83% on Nigerian speech ([arXiv 2511.14255](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.14255v1)). Expect 25–35% WER at launch from any phone-sized model. Plan a Nigerian-English fine-tune of the chosen streaming model. No public Zipformer fine-tune for Nigerian English exists, so that is genuine R&D. VAD gating (Silero about 2 MB, TEN VAD about 306 KB ([TEN VAD](https://github.com/TEN-framework/ten-vad))) curbs silence hallucination and saves battery in long meetings. sherpa-onnx's streaming English punctuation model restores punctuation for Zipformer output ([sherpa-onnx punctuation](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/punctuation/pretrained_models.html)).

The integration shape matters as much as the model. XDcobra's `react-native-sherpa-onnx` (MIT, bundles sherpa-onnx 1.12.35, Android API 24+, iOS 13+, Expo config plugin) offers streaming STT with endpointing, native mic capture and a model download manager. However, VAD is not due until 0.7.0, the project has about 45 stars, its iOS XCFramework is about 80 MB, and it bundles LGPL FFmpeg ([GitHub](https://github.com/XDcobra/react-native-sherpa-onnx)). Android 14+ requires a microphone-type foreground service that is started while the app is in the foreground ([Android FGS](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types)). For 60-minute screen-off sessions, the safe design is a custom Expo native module. It runs capture, VAD, ASR and chunked diarization in Kotlin and Swift, and streams only text events to JavaScript. The binding is useful as a reference or fork base, not as a dependency to trust blindly.

## Piper is fastest, but GPL and voice licences make Kitten the cleaner default

For "Speak for me", Piper remains the speed leader. On a Galaxy S10, **Piper low voices ran at RTF 0.076–0.077 with about 0.5 s median latency**, close to Android system TTS at 0.058 ([VoicePing](https://voiceping.net/en/blog/research-offline-tts-eval/)). x_low and low voices are about 20–28 MB ([piper-voices](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/raw/293cad0539066f86e6bce3b9780c472cc9157489/voices.json)). It carries three legal traps, though. Active development moved to the GPL-3.0 `OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl` repo, and the README says it "embeds espeak-ng" ([piper1-gpl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl/main/README.md)). Piper grants no rights over voices: anything fine-tuned from lessac or ryan, or trained on non-commercial data, is not commercially usable, and older downloads with the same file name do not qualify after the late-2026 retrains ([COMMERCIAL_USE.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl/main/docs/COMMERCIAL_USE.md)). Most important, **espeak-ng (GPL-3.0) is statically linked into sherpa-onnx's Android and iOS prebuilt libraries**, so it ships whichever model you load. One reviewer flagged that GPLv3 conflicts with App Store terms ([rtchat #1500](https://github.com/muxable/rtchat/issues/1500)). That team resolved it by writing a native ONNX Runtime engine and dropping sherpa-onnx and espeak entirely ([rtchat #1502](https://github.com/muxable/rtchat/issues/1502)).

Kitten TTS v0.8 is the cleanest English default. Its weights are Apache-2.0. On an x86 emulator, nano fp32 (78 MB) gave 240 ms time-to-first-audio, RTF 0.093, +186 MB RSS, and the best WER of the English models tested (12.1%, against Piper's 21.9%) ([rtchat #1500](https://github.com/muxable/rtchat/issues/1500)). Real-phone numbers are less flattering. An older Kitten nano ran at **RTF 0.387 on the Galaxy S10** ([VoicePing](https://voiceping.net/en/blog/research-offline-tts-eval/)), still well under real time but five times slower than Piper. Kitten TTS 2 nano and pico are 4–17 MB per language, and pico claims faster-than-real-time on one phone CPU thread with 40–50 MB peak memory. But they sit under an unread "Stellon Labs Community License" ([KittenTTS](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS)). Supertonic 3 (145 MB int8, RTF about 0.2 on the emulator, 31 languages, no espeak at runtime) is a multilingual option. Its OpenRAIL-M licence passes use restrictions to users and requires disclosing output as machine-generated, and it emits audio only once per message ([rtchat #1500](https://github.com/muxable/rtchat/issues/1500)).

Kokoro-82M has the best quality and a clean Apache-2.0 licence ([hexgrad](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M)). But on the S10 it ran at **RTF 1.13 fp32 and 2.42 int8**, with 8–15 s median latency ([VoicePing](https://voiceping.net/en/blog/research-offline-tts-eval/)). Its int8 build sometimes emits garbage audio on ARM ([rtchat #1500](https://github.com/muxable/rtchat/issues/1500)). It belongs only as an opt-in Pro voice. Several models are ruled out on licence grounds: XTTS-v2 (CPML, non-commercial, and Coqui is defunct ([XTTS-v2](https://huggingface.co/coqui/XTTS-v2/commits/v2.0.3/LICENSE.txt))) and MMS-TTS (CC-BY-NC ([facebook/mms-tts-yor](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-tts-yor))). None of the permissive families ships a Nigerian-accented voice. YarnGPT has 11 Nigerian-accent voices under Apache-2.0, but at about 360M LLM parameters it is too heavy for real-time phone TTS ([YarnGPT](https://huggingface.co/saheedniyi/YarnGPT)). The compliant route to a Nigerian voice is to record a contracted speaker for a few hours, which Voxa then owns, and fine-tune a Piper voice from a CC0 base or a Kitten model. To get first audio under 1 s, split text on clause punctuation, since all candidates synthesize per sentence or per message ([rtchat #1500](https://github.com/muxable/rtchat/issues/1500)). Keep native OS TTS as the zero-download emergency fallback.

## Speaker labels are reliable after the meeting, approximate during it

sherpa-onnx's diarization pipeline is small enough for every tier. **pyannote segmentation-3.0 is 1.5 MB int8, and paired with NeMo TitaNet-small it runs at RTF 0.110 on one CPU thread**, against 0.24–0.30 with the 3D-Speaker embedder ([sherpa-onnx diarization](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/speaker-diarization/models.html)). Prebuilt APKs exist for armeabi-v7a as well as arm64 ([sherpa-onnx APKs](https://huggingface.co/csukuangfj/sherpa-onnx-apk/tree/a2f7eea6eec13c56ef360e46a943e7c2f9b7da12/speaker-diarization/1.10.32)). The pipeline clusters over a complete file, so it is batch-only. Avoid the Reverb segmentation model, which is non-commercial. If a low-end core is 2–4x slower than the desktop reference, a 60-minute meeting would take roughly 15–30 minutes to diarize afterwards. That too is an estimate. Live labels therefore need custom work: diarize each finished 30–60 s chunk, link speakers across chunks by embedding centroids, then re-cluster the whole meeting at the end to replace the provisional labels.

True streaming diarization exists but is uneven across platforms. On iOS, FluidAudio (Apache-2.0, official React Native wrapper) ships LS-EEND streaming for up to 10 speakers and a Sortformer option, though some models need iOS 18+ and no iPhone speed is published ([FluidAudio](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio)). On Android, the only candidate is a community ONNX port of NVIDIA Streaming Sortformer (117M params, 4 speakers max), which is a Pro-tier experiment at best ([NVIDIA](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/diar_streaming_sortformer_4spk-v2.1)). Accuracy should be set honestly in the UI. Even Sortformer reaches **20.57% DER on single-distant-mic AMI and 36.76% on NOTSOFAR1 meetings with five or more speakers** ([NVIDIA](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/diar_streaming_sortformer_4spk-v2.1)). pyannote 3.1 scores 22.4% on AMI array audio ([pyannote](https://huggingface.co/pyannote/speaker-diarization-3.1)). A phone on a table in a noisy Lagos meeting room should be expected to land around 18–25% DER for three or four speakers. Voxa therefore needs speaker rename and merge, plus an optional "how many people?" prompt. No diarization evaluation exists for Nigerian English or Pidgin.

## Platform OCR wins "Scan", but Yoruba and Igbo diacritics are untested

For Scan, no third-party model beats the platform engines on cost. **Bundled ML Kit Text Recognition v2 adds about 4 MB per script per architecture and works offline immediately**. The unbundled 260 KB variant fetches its model through Play Services ([ML Kit](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/text-recognition/android?authuser=0)), which is a poor bet for a first launch offline. Delivering per-ABI APKs through App Bundles keeps the bundled cost from multiplying ([ML Kit app size](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/tips/reduce-app-size?authuser=8)). On iOS, Apple Vision's `RecognizeTextRequest` is free, and its supported-language list must be queried at runtime ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/recognizetextrequest.md)). The catch is that ML Kit lists Hausa, Igbo and Yoruba only as "mapped" to the Latin model, not as supported ([ML Kit v2 languages](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/text-recognition/v2/languages)). Underdots and tone marks may be dropped. For Lite, a still capture followed by one recognition call avoids frame-processor and worklet dependencies. Vision Camera plugins such as `react-native-vision-camera-mlkit` can add live OCR on better tiers ([GitHub](https://github.com/pedrol2b/react-native-vision-camera-mlkit)). PaddleOCR mobile was not researched and remains a fallback to evaluate if diacritic tests fail.

## Extractive summaries everywhere; a sub-1 GB LLM for Smart

Quick summaries need no model download. TextRank scores **35.25 ROUGE-1 on AMI meetings** ([arXiv 1805.05271](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.05271)), BM25 weighting lifts that to 37.39 ([arXiv 1606.07829](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07829)), and ClusterRank beats both on spontaneous speech ([Interspeech 2009](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2009/garg09_interspeech.html)). Combined with MMR de-duplication and rule-based "possible action items" (commitment phrases, second-person requests, date expressions), this is the Lite summary. It should also appear instantly on every tier while Smart runs, or when Smart runs out of memory.

For Smart, the 2026 field favours Apache-2.0 models under 1 GB. **Qwen3.5 0.8B and 2B** (Apache-2.0, March 2026, with LiteRT-LM conversions) are the lowest-risk picks ([HF Qwen3.5-0.8B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-0.8B); [LiteRT 2B](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/Qwen3.5-2B)). Gemma 3 1B int4 is a proven 529 MB fallback ([HF](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/Gemma3-1B-IT)). LFM2.5-1.2B is the speed leader: 70 tok/s decode at 719 MB on an S25 Ultra CPU ([Liquid AI](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/introducing-lfm2-5-the-next-generation-of-on-device-ai)) and about 31 tok/s on a Pixel 8a ([HF](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/LFM2.5-1.2B-Instruct)). But its licence caps commercial use for organizations above US$10M revenue. Gemma 4 E2B (Apache-2.0) is excellent on flagship GPUs, with 52 tok/s decode and 676 MB peak on the S26 Ultra. Its file is 2.58 GB, though ([HF](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/gemma-4-E2B-it-litert-lm)), which is about ₦1,300–2,000 of data. That makes it a Pro-only Wi-Fi download.

A 60-minute meeting is about 13–14K tokens. That is beyond the 4K windows of Apple Foundation Models ([TN3193](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/technotes/tn3193-managing-the-on-device-foundation-model-s-context-window.md)) and ML Kit GenAI ([ML Kit](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/genai/summarization/android)). Every path therefore needs map-reduce: summarize 3–4K-token chunks into JSON key points and action items, then merge. On a mid-range CPU at roughly 200 tok/s prefill and 15–25 tok/s decode, a 1B model should finish in about 2–3 minutes. That is derived, not measured. Mid-range Cortex-A76/A77 phones decode only 2–4 tok/s on models above 1B ([arXiv 2410.03613](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03613v5)), which is why Smart should not run on Lite. Turn off Qwen's "thinking" mode, since non-reasoning models did better on meeting summarization ([arXiv 2507.02145](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.02145)). For the runtime, llama.rn with GGUF gives the widest React Native coverage, with Metal on iOS and OpenCL on Adreno 700+ ([llama.rn README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/cui-llama.rn@1.12.2/README.md)). react-native-executorch has the friendliest Expo `useLLM` hook and a curated model registry ([SWM docs](https://docs.swmansion.com/react-native-executorch/docs/hooks/natural-language-processing/useLLM)). LiteRT-LM is fastest for Gemma 4 but has no React Native binding. Apple Foundation Models and Gemini Nano are zero-download bonuses on the small flagship share ([ML Kit GenAI](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/genai)).

## Recommended stack per tier

The device mix drives the tiering. Nigeria is **86.58% Android** ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/nigeria)). Transsion took 65% of Q2 2025 shipments ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/?p=167210)), and entry phones such as the itel A50 still ship with 2–3 GB RAM ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/05/29/the-best-itel-phone-in-nigeria-for-2025/)). About 85% of Android usage is on Android 11–16 ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/android-version-market-share/mobile-tablet/nigeria)), so Android 8–10 is a real but shrinking tail. Expo SDK 58 still supports Android 7+ ([Expo](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/)). Tier by `expo-device` `totalMemory`, using about 3.5 GB and 6.5 GB cutoffs because kernel-visible RAM is below the advertised figure, plus arm64 availability ([expo-device](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/device/)). Then confirm the tier with a 5–10 s first-run micro-benchmark of ASR RTF, so that slow Unisoc chips with generous RAM are demoted. These thresholds are proposals to validate, not sourced facts.

| Feature | Lite (under 3.5 GB, or no arm64, or slow benchmark) | Standard (3.5–6.5 GB, arm64) | Pro (6.5 GB+, or recent iPhone) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listen (ASR) | Zipformer 20M int8 (~42 MB) or 2023-06-26 int8 (~70 MB), TEN/Silero VAD, online punct int8; bundled | Zipformer 2023-06-21 int8 (~180 MB) or Moonshine v2 Small, or NeMo FastConformer 114M; Wi-Fi download | Nemotron Streaming 0.6B int4 (0.67 GB, Wi-Fi only); iOS 26 SpeechTranscriber where available; optional whisper.rn re-pass |
| Speaker labels | pyannote-3.0 int8 + TitaNet-small, after the meeting (or while charging) | Same, plus provisional per-chunk labels during the meeting | Chunked live labels on Android; FluidAudio LS-EEND on iOS; Sortformer ONNX experiment |
| Speak for me | Licence-clean Piper x_low/low or Kitten v0.8 nano int8 (~25 MB); native OS TTS fallback | Kitten v0.8 nano fp32 (~56–78 MB) or Piper medium (~63 MB) | Add opt-in Kokoro q8 (~92 MB); Supertonic 3 if multilingual and OpenRAIL-M accepted |
| Summary | Quick only: TextRank/ClusterRank + BM25, MMR, rule-based action items | Quick + Smart opt-in: Qwen3.5 0.8B Q4 (~0.5 GB) or Gemma 3 1B int4, 3–4K map-reduce | Qwen3.5 2B Q4 (~1.2 GB) or Gemma 4 E2B on GPU; Apple FM / Gemini Nano where present |
| Scan | Bundled ML Kit Latin, still capture; Apple Vision on iOS | Same, plus live frame OCR | Same, plus LLM diacritic correction (future) |

Two model-delivery rules follow from data prices of about ₦575 per GB in 2025 ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/02/09/how-nigerians-spent-%E2%82%A620-87billion-daily-on-data-in-2025/)). Only Lite models go in the AAB/IPA. Everything else is an optional Wi-Fi download, with the size shown in MB and naira. Grid supply below 3,500 MW, with many states getting under 12 hours a day ([Leadership](https://leadership.ng/power-supply-dips-further-amid-gas-shortages-low-water-levels-n6trn-debt/)), argues for a "process later" queue. Full re-diarization and Smart summaries should run while charging or above a battery threshold.

## Licences, unmeasured phones and Nigerian data are the real risks

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| espeak-ng GPL-3.0 in sherpa-onnx prebuilts | Ships with every model; GPLv3 sits poorly with closed-source App Store distribution ([rtchat #1500](https://github.com/muxable/rtchat/issues/1500)) | Build sherpa-onnx without espeak-ng (no documented flag found), use an espeak-free phonemizer (misaki with `fallback=None` ([misaki](https://github.com/hexgrad/misaki)), a lexicon plus small G2P), use espeak-free models (Supertonic), or get legal sign-off |
| Piper voice provenance | lessac/ryan-derived voices are not commercial; same-named older files do not qualify ([COMMERCIAL_USE.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl/main/docs/COMMERCIAL_USE.md)) | Pin voices by MODEL_CARD hash; prefer CC0 rdh bases; train an owned Nigerian voice |
| Non-commercial Nigerian data and models | AfriSpeech-200 and NaijaVoices are CC BY-NC-SA ([arXiv 2511.14255](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.14255); [HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/naijavoices/naijavoices-dataset)); MMS is CC-BY-NC ([HF](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-1b/commit/a2946eb16e55841b17a7684a9576dc67dc9a885f)) | Train on WAXAL (CC-BY-4.0 ([Google](https://research.google/blog/waxal-a-large-scale-open-resource-for-african-language-speech-technology/))), BibleTTS (CC BY-SA ([OpenSLR 129](https://www.openslr.org/129))), Common Voice and owned recordings; negotiate AfriSpeech/NaijaVoices licences |
| Unmeasured low-end performance | No RTF, RAM, battery or thermal data on 2 GB Android 8 phones for any engine | The validation plan below; ship nothing on Lite until it passes |
| Thin React Native bindings | react-native-sherpa-onnx is ~45 stars, lacks VAD, bundles LGPL FFmpeg ([GitHub](https://github.com/XDcobra/react-native-sherpa-onnx)) | Own a custom Expo module around sherpa-onnx's Kotlin/Swift APIs |
| Licence fine print elsewhere | Kitten 2 (Stellon), LFM (US$10M cap), Supertonic (OpenRAIL-M), Sortformer/Nemotron (NVIDIA Open Model), N-ATLAS (1,000-user cap ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/09/25/nigerian-government-awarri-launch-n-atlas/))), gated pyannote weights | One legal review covering every shipped weight file before launch |

Future Yoruba, Hausa and Igbo support should be sequenced by difficulty. **Hausa is easiest**: an MIT-licensed Piper voice trained on WAXAL already exists. It is warm-started from lessac, though, so check its chain, and it uses graphemes because espeak-ng has no Hausa voice ([HF](https://huggingface.co/adab-tech/murya-piper-hausa-tts)). BibleTTS also has 86.6 aligned Hausa hours. Yoruba needs an on-device diacritic restorer before TTS, because undiacritized words such as "igba" carry four meanings ([arXiv 2610.01634](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.01634)). Yoruba ASR should output text without tone marks by default, since models do better that way ([LoResLM 2026](https://preview.aclanthology.org/new-sigs/2026.loreslm-1.14/)). Igbo is hardest: 0.4 validated Common Voice hours ([MDC](https://datacollective.mozillafoundation.org/datasets/cmj8u3p9a00c5nxxb7jwi217j)) and 30.5% WER even for the 2B NaijaVox-2.0 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Axiveri/NaijaVox-2.0)). Meta's Omnilingual ASR (Apache-2.0, 300M CTC variant) is the most promising commercial multilingual base, but it is non-streaming and about 1.3 GiB in fp32 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/facebook/omniASR-LLM-7B/blob/main/README.md)). Natural English–Yoruba code-switching breaks Whisper, MMS and Omnilingual zero-shot ([NeurIPS 2026 YECS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/179704)).

### Validation plan: benchmark on six phones before committing

Buy or borrow a panel that mirrors the market:

- **Lite floor**: an itel A50 (2 GB) and a Tecno Pop 10.
- **One true Android 8 or 9, 2 GB device, ideally 32-bit-only**: this settles whether armeabi-v7a support is worth keeping.
- **Standard**: a Tecno Spark 40 or Infinix Hot and a Redmi 14C. All of these are among Nigeria's most-searched 2025 phones ([Techeconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/most-searched-smartphones-in-nigeria-for-2025/)).
- **Pro**: a Snapdragon 7-class or better Android (Adreno 700+), an iPhone 11 to probe SpeechTranscriber availability, and a current iPhone.

Run all tests in release builds of the custom module, at 2 and 4 threads, with int8 and fp32 compared on ARM. The emulator showed int8 slower than fp32 on x86, and that needs re-checking on ARM ([rtchat #1500](https://github.com/muxable/rtchat/issues/1500)).

Measure these five things, against proposed internal targets that are not sourced:

1. **ASR**: RTF, partial-result latency and peak RSS for each candidate. For Lite, target RTF ≤ 0.5 and ≤ 300 MB, which leaves headroom for diarization.
2. **ASR accuracy**: WER on an owned 5–10 hour Nigerian test set of real meetings, Pidgin mixing, names and numbers. Report AfriSpeech-200 test and Med-Conv-Nig only as reference, given their NC terms.
3. **The 60-minute screen-off meeting**: battery drop per hour (target ≤ 15%), thermal throttling, foreground-service survival on Transsion's HiOS/XOS, and iOS background-audio survival. No source covered the iOS side.
4. **Diarization**: time to diarize a 60-minute file, and DER on 10 owned recordings with 2, 4 and 6 speakers.
5. **TTS, OCR and summaries**: TTS time-to-first-audio (target < 1 s with clause chunking) plus a listening panel of Nigerian users. OCR character accuracy on printed and handwritten Yoruba/Igbo text with diacritics. Smart-summary wall-clock time and peak RAM for a 14K-token transcript.

In parallel, settle three questions:

- Can sherpa-onnx be built without espeak-ng for both iOS and Android?
- Do Kitten 2 and the chosen Piper voices clear legal review?
- What would AfriSpeech and NaijaVoices commercial licences cost?

Decide Lite models only after the floor devices pass. Any model that misses its target there moves up a tier rather than shipping degraded.

## Conclusion

The research changes where Voxa's effort should go. Choosing models is the easy part, because the small, permissive, cross-platform options already converge on sherpa-onnx, platform OCR, extractive summaries and a sub-1 GB Apache-2.0 LLM. The defensible product work lies in three places no off-the-shelf component covers. The first is an espeak-free, licence-clean speech stack. The second is a Nigerian-English acoustic and voice layer built on commercially usable or owned data, which alone can close a 13–20x accuracy gap. The third is a native background pipeline that survives an hour-long meeting on a 2 GB Transsion phone during a power cut.

Two implications follow. First, the "Smart on capable phones" framing is right but should be triggered by measured device capability, not RAM alone, because Nigeria's market mixes generous-RAM phones with slow chips. Second, owned data is strategic. A few hours of contracted Nigerian voice recordings and a modest in-house meeting test set unlock a local voice, honest accuracy claims and a fine-tuning path at once. Hausa, then Yoruba, then Igbo is the natural order for adding languages on top of that foundation.
